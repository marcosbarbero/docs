# Second Chance

> One revive per run, offered only when you die close to something. Free players watch a short video; Premium players simply carry on.

## What it does

Dying two fruit from a level target, or ninety points from your best, is the moment a run is worth rescuing. Second Chance offers exactly that — once.

## When it appears

- **Classic:** when your score reaches **90% of your personal best**. Never on your first game, when there is no best to be close to.
- **Campaign:** when you are **within two fruit** of the level's target.

It is deliberately not offered on a run that was nowhere near. An offer that appears every time teaches people to dismiss it unread, and then it is worth nothing on the run where it mattered.

## Free vs Premium

|  | Free | Premium |
|---|---|---|
| When it is offered | near miss only | near miss only — **same rule** |
| Revives per run | 1 | 1 — **same rule** |
| What it costs | a short video | nothing |
| If no ad is available | no offer | offered anyway |
| What you do | tap, watch, dismiss | nothing; it continues on its own |

**Paying changes the cost, never the rules.** Premium does not grant more second chances or a looser condition — a personal best built on five revives is a hollow record, and that is worth protecting for everyone.

If the video fails to load *after* you agreed to watch it, you get the revive anyway. An empty ad slot is our problem, not yours.

## What a revive does

The snake restarts at the middle of the board with your score, fruit count and speed intact. It does not resume from where you died, for a simple reason: that cell may be a wall, an obstacle or your own body, so continuing from it is not always a legal position at all — and reviving into the exact situation that just killed you is a second chance in name only.

## Known limits

- One per run. Taking it twice would make the score board meaningless.
- The free version needs an ad to be available; where none is, no offer is shown rather than a button that promises something undeliverable.
